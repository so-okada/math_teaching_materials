\[V_{\mathrm{inside}}(L)=\pi\int_1^L\frac{dx}{x^2}=\pi\left(1-\frac1L\right).\]

\[V_{\mathrm{coat}}(L,t)=\pi\int_1^L\left[\left(\frac1x+t\right)^2-\frac1{x^2}\right]dx=2\pi t\ln L+\pi t^2(L-1).\]

Coating volume, term by term: from \(2\pi t\ln L\) + from \(\pi t^2(L-1)\), in cubic units.

Surface area and coating thickness

The area of the horn’s curved side uses arc length: \(ds=\sqrt{1+x^{-4}}\,dx\). It excludes the end disks and does not depend on the coating thickness.

\[A(L)=2\pi\int_1^L\frac{\sqrt{1+x^{-4}}}{x}\,dx.\]

Lateral surface area A(L) · square units
Radial thin-layer limit · square units\(2\pi\ln L\)
Radial volume ÷ t · square units\(2\pi\ln L+\pi t(L-1)\)

\[2\pi\ln L\leq A(L)\leq 2\pi\ln L+\frac{\pi}{4}(1-L^{-4}).\]

These bounds follow from \(1\leq\sqrt{1+u}\leq 1+u/2\) for \(u\geq0\). Thus the surface area diverges logarithmically, while the interior volume approaches π. For L > 1, the area exceeds \(2\pi\ln L\) by less than π/4, so the two curves lie close together.

Horizontal axis: log₁₀ L. Vertical axis: square units, logarithmic scale; values below 10⁻² sit on the bottom edge. All three quantities are zero at L = 1.

Radial thickness increases the radius at the same x. Even for a fixed \(L>1\), dividing its volume by t and letting t decrease gives \(2\pi\ln L\), not \(A(L)\).

Normal thickness is measured perpendicular to the surface. For a fixed finite segment, a sufficiently thin normal layer of thickness h has volume approximately \(hA(L)\): its volume divided by h tends to \(A(L)\) as \(h\to0^+\). This describes a layer on the curved side, with no coating on the end disks. The 3D view retains the radial model.
